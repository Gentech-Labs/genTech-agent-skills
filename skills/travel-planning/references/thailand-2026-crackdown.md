## Proof of Funds

**Updated 2026-09-23 from the official sources (TAT + Royal Thai Embassy Washington).**

The rule is real but it is NOT "cash or nothing". The official wording (TAT, 6 July 2026)
says visitors "may be asked to show funds in Thai Baht, an equivalent amount in another
currency, **or documents evidencing payment of an equivalent amount**."

| Category | Per person | Per family |
|---|---|---|
| Transit visa / certain visa-exempt cases | 10,000 THB (~$301) | 20,000 THB |
| Visa on Arrival | 10,000 THB (~$301) | 20,000 THB |
| Tourist visa / Non-immigrant | 20,000 THB (~$601) | 40,000 THB |

Does not apply to children under 12. Rate used: 1 USD = 33.258 THB (our own fx tracker,
2026-09-22), so 20,000 THB = **$601**, not the ~$550 widely quoted from an older rate.

**⚠️ Ambiguity worth knowing.** TAT's own page lists "20,000 Baht" only for *Tourist Visa*
and *Non-Immigrant Visa*, and lists 10,000 Baht for *"certain visa-exempt cases under
immigration regulations"* — without defining which. The Royal Thai Embassy in Washington's
visa-exemption page says 20,000 Baht applies to the Tourist Visa Exemption Scheme. **The
embassy's reading is the accepted one in practice: plan for 20,000 THB ($601).**

**Format — the nuance most guides get wrong:**
- **Cash is the reliable answer.** Any currency at the equivalent value is accepted — THB,
  USD, EUR — so you do NOT have to buy baht in advance. USD cash at $600+ satisfies it.
- **Bank-app screenshots are the weak form.** Many travellers get away with them; officers
  are not *obligated* to accept them, and enforcement tightened in 2026 under the "No Entry,
  No Stay, No Escape" policy. Treat an app screen as a backup, never the plan.
- **A printed bank statement / passbook / credit-card statement is explicitly named by the
  official wording** as acceptable evidence ("documents evidencing payment"). Better than a
  screenshot, weaker than cash.
- **Best play: cash on your person + a printed statement as backup.** Not in checked luggage.

**Enforcement is discretionary and random.** Officers have full discretion and the final
decision on entry rests with them. Checks are spot checks — people are pulled from the line.
The TAT reminder (6 July 2026) exists because enforcement resumed after a couple of quiet
years; it is a reminder of a rule that has existed since 1980, not a new law.

## Overstay Penalties

| Penalty | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Fine | 500 Baht/day, capped at 20,000 Baht (~$550) |
| Jail | Up to 2 years (statutory) |
| Re-entry ban | 1-10 years depending on overstay length |
| Arrested while overstaying | Mandatory deportation + 5-10 year ban |

## Unruly Tourist Crackdown (May 2026)

PM Anutin Charnvirakul ordered nationwide enforcement:
- **Zero tolerance** in Phuket — maximum penalties, immediate visa revocation
- **No driving without valid license** — court appearance without exception
- **Cultural respect enforced** — no bullying locals, no disrespectful behavior
- **Nominee businesses investigated** — foreigners using tourist visas to work

## Mandatory Health Insurance (Proposed)

Thailand considering mandatory health insurance for all foreign visitors. Not yet implemented but being reviewed. Get travel insurance regardless.
