# Destination Entry Requirements — the reusable framework

**Why this exists (Jordan, 2026-09-27):** *"this is also stuff we should have built into the GenTech travels
because this is all handy stuff anybody would need to know before they choose a location."*

**The need is the unit, not the app.** So this is not a Thailand note — it is the **nine questions every
destination must answer before a trip is chosen**, with Thailand filled in as the worked example. A destination
without answers is not "fine by default"; it is **unverified**.

> **Never answer these from memory or a blog.** Fares, visa rules and entry conditions change, and a wrong
> entry rule strands someone at a border. Verify against an official source and record the date.

---

## The nine questions

| # | Question | Why it strands people |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **How long can I stay, and for what purpose?** | Purpose matters as much as length — some exemptions are *tourism only* |
| 2 | **What proof of funds, if any?** | Usually a standing condition, enforced at an officer's discretion |
| 3 | **Proof of onward/return travel?** | Among the most commonly enforced, and easy to forget |
| 4 | **Digital arrival card / pre-registration?** | Often mandatory, with a short window that opens only days before |
| 5 | **Is there an autogate/e-gate, and can I actually use it?** | Departure automation ≠ arrival automation |
| 6 | **Passport validity rule** | Usually 6 months beyond entry; a hard, unbending gate |
| 7 | **Season and weather reality** | Peak season and rainy season pull prices in opposite directions |
| 8 | **Island/regional side-effects** | A whole coast can be rough in a month that's fine elsewhere |
| 9 | **Currency and what "cash equivalent" means in practice** | What an officer will *accept* is narrower than what you *have* |

## Worked example — 🇹🇭 THAILAND (verified 2026-09-27)

**1. Length + purpose.** US citizens: **30 days visa-free, tourism only.** ⚠️ **Changed 15 September 2026** —
Thailand **ended the 60-day exemption** (four Royal Gazette orders, 31 Aug 2026; 60 countries now at 30 days).
Land-border entries were tightened separately.

**2. Proof of funds.** **Real, and written into the exemption itself** — which is why two conflicting
descriptions circulate online (both are partly right: it's a standing rule *enforced at discretion*).

- **Tourist visa exemption: 20,000 THB per person / 40,000 per family** ← the standard tourist route
- Visa on arrival: 10,000 / 20,000 · Transit/other exemption cases: 10,000 / 20,000
- **~$599 per person / ~$1,198 family** at 33.39 THB/USD (2026-09-27); ≈ 35,651 DOP

**🔑 It does not have to be cash.** Accepted: *"funds in Thai baht, an equivalent amount in another currency,
**or documents proving payment or access to an equivalent amount**."*

> **The practical trap:** a bank statement is clearly "documents". **A crypto balance is not obviously
> "documents proving access" to an immigration officer.** Carry the boring artifact.

**3. Onward travel.** Required under the exemption, and **more important since the 60→30 cut**, not less.

**4. TDAC — mandatory.** All non-Thai nationals must complete the **Thailand Digital Arrival Card online within
72 hours before arrival** (mandatory since May 2026). It **cannot be submitted earlier than 3 days out** — so
it's a thing you do in the window, not prep.

**5. Autogates — the nuance that matters.** As of **9 Sep 2026**:
- **Departure** e-gates for foreign e-passports: ✅ **fully live** at BKK/DMK
- **Arrival** Auto Gates: ⚠️ **expansion still in progress** — approved in principle 17 Aug 2026 for **31 more
  nationalities**, implementation *"depends on equipment and security-system"*
- Published guidance: a **first-time visitor, a new passport, or a record needing checks may still need a
  staffed counter**

> **Returning visitor vs first-timer is the real variable.** A **repeat visitor** who has previously entered
> and enrolled biometrics is the better autogate candidate. Being a repeat visitor is a **genuine** reason to
> expect the gate — but it is still not a guarantee, so **carry the proof anyway.** (This corrected our own
> 2026-09-27 note, which assumed a first-time visitor.)

**6. Passport.** 6+ months validity beyond entry.

**7. Season.** **Nov–Feb is PEAK** — prices rise. Rainy season pulls the other way. **The two forces oppose
each other, so a "cheaper because of floods" thesis is unproven — measure with real fares.** Slightly favours
late October over late November if price decides.

**8. Regional side-effect.** The **Gulf side (Koh Samui, Koh Phangan) can be rough in November–December** — a
surprise when the rest of the country is fine.

**9. Currency.** Cash is still king outside malls; Grab/BTS/MRT in Bangkok. Tipping not expected (20–50 THB).

## Pitfalls

- **Never answer from memory or a blog** — verify at an official source and date the finding.
- **A standing rule ≠ a guaranteed ask, and ≠ a rule that can't be enforced.** Both framings mislead.
- **Departure automation is not arrival automation.** Travellers report one and assume the other.
- **"Documents proving access" is narrower than "I have money."** Think like an officer.
- **Purpose limits bite silently** — a *tourism-only* exemption is not a generic 30-day stay.
- **Peak season and weather pull opposite ways.** Don't accept a one-directional price story.
- **Regional side-effects** — check the specific coast/island, not the country average.
