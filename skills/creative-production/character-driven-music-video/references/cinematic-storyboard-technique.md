# Cinematic Storyboard Technique — Reference

## What Changed This Session

Vanito discovered the **per-second scene description technique** from a NEXUS tweet about Seedance 2.0 prompts. Instead of writing a single caption, the technique breaks each 5-second clip into a mini film direction with:

- Camera movement (push-in, track, dolly, pan, tilt, zoom)
- Character blocking (where they are, how they move)
- Lighting cues (pulses, shifts, color changes)
- Sound cues (what the audience hears)
- Lyrics sync (which lyrics play during this scene)
- Mood (emotional tone)

## The Key Insight

> "I wanted you to create a whole animation out of that... like a whole film... keeping those characters still in the film like they're the characters and just create a whole animation out of it."

This is the difference between:
- **Before**: "KAGE plays guitar in a red room" → random output
- **After**: "0-5s: KAGE's tattooed fingers strike the first chord. Hard. The strings vibrate. His spiky black hair shakes as he throws his head back. Slow push-in on his face. Red light pulses." → directed, cinematic output

## The NEXUS Example (from the tweet)

```
0–3 seconds: Establish the spatial problem. A woman enters a dark workshop in the background while a suspended machine dominates the foreground.
3–6 seconds: She moves diagonally toward the machine. The camera tracks sideways, allowing foreground chains to repeatedly conceal and reveal her.
6–10 seconds: She reaches beneath it. A mechanical sound comes from behind her. She stops before turning. The camera stops with her.
10–13 seconds: Cut behind the machine. Her face is now framed through its internal structure as something moves in the distant doorway.
13–15 seconds: She turns toward the doorway. The machine abruptly activates in the foreground, flooding her silhouette with red light. Cut.
```

## The TechHalla Example (Midjourney prompt structure)

The second image showed a **hyper-detailed production still prompt** with:
- **DIRECTIVE**: "high-impact blockbuster cinematic still"
- **SUBJECT / CHARACTER DEFINITIONS**: detailed physical descriptions of each character
- **SCENE & LAYOUT**: camera position, environment, lighting
- **TECHNICAL SPECS**: camera model, film stock, color science, texture, composition

This is the image-generation equivalent of the Seedance per-second technique — treat every frame like a film production.
